Three buckets: bring them in, sell them more, build what they need. Everything comes in one way and is kept as activity on the client. Every piece of work, in any bucket or anywhere else in the back office, is a to-do on one list. Reggie does most of them; the ones that need a person come to you.

Three pieces, one to-do list

The pipelines aren't a separate tool. They sit on two things the whole back office shares: activity (what happened) and to-dos (what someone has to do). A card in a pipeline is just the thing the activity and to-dos are about.

What happened

Activity

Every email, message, call, meeting note, question, answer, run, sign-off and change, on one timeline per client (and per lead before they're a client). Kept, never deleted.

A client's question is activity: answered and logged, not a ticket.
What someone has to do

To-do

One list for the whole back office. Each to-do has an owner (you, Reggie or the client), a due time, why it exists, and the thing it's about. Done when the work is done, not when it's ticked.

"Approve the fix for Hartley's Ferguson bills", due today, yours.
What it's about

Cards

Deals, opportunities, issues, agent builds and enhancements: the pipelines' cards. A card's stage moves when its to-dos are done; its timeline is the activity about it.

03

Sell them more: expansion

Reggie watches every live client each night and opens an opportunity when it sees one, with the evidence from their own data. You decide which to pursue. Contracts and billing changes are by hand for now: you agree the terms and record them on the card.

What Reggie looks for

  • They ask for something we don't do. An enhancement that is really a new agent comes here.
  • Work we see but don't handle. Emails and documents forwarded that no agent picks up; a business area with data and no agent (a bank feed, no Bookkeeping).
  • What similar clients use. Agents that clients of the same kind and size have and this one doesn't.
  • Growing past their plan. Volume up for three months running.
  • Renewal coming. 60 days ahead, with a summary of what we did for them.

What it doesn't do

  • Pitch to a client with an open critical or high issue. Fix first, sell after.
  • Pitch to a client whose health is at risk. That's a check-in, for you.
  • Promise a price or a date. Those are yours.
  • Send an offer without your OK.

Issues

Something that isn't working as it should. Reggie often finds them before the client does: failed or stuck runs, more rejected writes than usual, a connection down. The first thing it does is triage the urgency.

The safety hold

  • What it stops: the agent's writes to the client's tools and its messages out (email, texts, calls). Runs keep going as drafts, so nothing is lost: what it would have done waits, held.
  • For security: also the connections involved, until you've looked. The client's own tools aren't touched.
  • Who: Reggie puts it on by itself for a critical issue (it only makes things safer). Only you take it off: release, release after a fix, or roll back to an earlier version.
  • Who's told: you at once (a phone notification), the client admin in plain words within the hour, and the hold is on the issue's timeline.
  • After: what was held is reviewed: sent as is, edited, or dropped.

Agreed

  • 1Set-up is the end of New account: the account is made and each agent's needs written down. Each agent build starts with its requirements request, the intake questionnaire sent as a form.
  • 2Contracts and billing changes are by hand first, recorded on the card with the signed copy. Later, a Billing page in the portal where clients add things and agree the changes we post.
  • 3Critical issues (business critical or security) go first. Other issues go before builds, unless a build would miss its promised date. Enhancements after.
  • 4A safety hold: Reggie stops an agent's writes and messages on a critical issue; only you release it.
  • 5Platform bugs become engineering issues with links to the evidence, never client data.
  • 6A question is activity, not a ticket, with a to-do that keeps it from getting lost.
  • 7One to-do list across the whole back office; the pipelines are built on it, not beside it.
